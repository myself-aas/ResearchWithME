Public reading notes are useful when they preserve the claim, its source, and the reason a conclusion changed. This reflective draft describes a personal workflow rather than a controlled productivity study. Its practical test is whether another reader can reconstruct an argument later without depending on the author’s memory or private bookmarks.

**Scope:** The reflections below are author-supplied personal observations, not a tracked study of productivity or publication outcomes. They should not be generalized to every writer.

## The private notebook problem

Private notes have no reader, and notes without a reader drift toward shorthand. Six months later, a
line like "check the ablation — suspicious" is worse than useless: it records that I had a thought
without recording what the thought was.

Writing for a public reader forces the missing sentence. Not because the reader needs it, but because
the act of writing it is how I discover whether I actually have it.

## What changed

Three things, in order of surprise:

1. **Fewer abandoned threads.** A thread I have written about is a thread I return to. Publication
   creates a small obligation that private notes never do.
2. **Slower conclusions.** Knowing someone might check the claim makes me look for the load-bearing
   assumption before I write the sentence. This has killed several conclusions I was fond of.
3. **A usable archive.** Search over my own writing now works better than search over my bookmarks,
   because the writing contains the reasoning rather than the URL.

## The parts that did not improve

Public writing also has costs worth naming. I second-guess hedged sentences more than I should. I have
caught myself softening a criticism because the author might read it — and then caught myself doing it
again. And there is a real temptation to publish the version of a finding that makes a cleaner post.

## The practice, concretely

Nothing elaborate. One folder of Markdown files, one post when a thread reaches a conclusion, and a
short list of open questions carried between posts.

The practice as a pathway — pick honestly, and the shortcuts accumulate:

<NotesPracticePathway />

The only unusual rule is that I do not publish a
post until I can state, in one paragraph, what I believed before reading and what I believe now. If the
two paragraphs are identical, there is no post yet.

That rule has saved me from publishing some drafts before publication. The remaining writing has benefited from a clearer argument, which is the part I did not expect.

## Reproduction playbook

**Proposed evaluation.** No new experiment is reported by this editorial revision.

1. Select one completed reading note and record the paper/repository version, page or line locator, the claim you initially believed and the evidence that changed it. Keep private or sensitive material out of the public record.

2. Ask a reader to reconstruct that claim without the original conversation. Record where context, definitions or counterevidence are missing; do not score writing quality by page views or the number of notes produced.

3. Revise the note and preserve the correction history. If tracking the practice over time, define what counts as an abandoned thread before comparing periods; retrospective recollection alone is not a causal estimate.

## Pre-publication audit

- [ ] Separate personal recollection from measured outcomes and universal advice.
- [ ] Keep sources and corrections recoverable without publishing private notes.
- [ ] Retain a counterargument even when it makes the narrative less tidy.

## Verdict

Use public notes as a way to expose reasoning and improve retrievability, not as a mandatory productivity intervention. The workflow is worth trying on one non-sensitive thread. Whether it improves completion or accuracy for another writer remains an empirical and personal question.
