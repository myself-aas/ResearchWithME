# Reporting guidelines: which one, and where to get it

Many journals require a completed reporting checklist with the submission. The checklists are published by the groups that maintain them and are free to download. This sheet tells you which one applies to your study design and where to find the official, current version. It does not reproduce the checklists; use the official files, because journals expect them and because they are revised from time to time.

## Find yours

| Study design | Guideline | Official source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Randomised trial | CONSORT | consort-spirit.org |
| Trial protocol | SPIRIT | consort-spirit.org |
| Systematic review or meta-analysis | PRISMA | prisma-statement.org |
| Protocol for a systematic review | PRISMA-P | prisma-statement.org |
| Cohort, case-control, or cross-sectional study | STROBE | strobe-statement.org |
| Diagnostic accuracy study | STARD | equator-network.org |
| Prediction model development or validation | TRIPOD | tripod-statement.org |
| Case report | CARE | care-statement.org |
| Qualitative research | SRQR or COREQ | equator-network.org |
| Quality improvement study | SQUIRE | squire-statement.org |
| Economic evaluation | CHEERS | equator-network.org |
| Animal research | ARRIVE | arriveguidelines.org |

The EQUATOR Network (equator-network.org) keeps a searchable library of all of these and of several hundred extensions for particular designs and fields. If your design is not in the table, search there. Web addresses change; if one of these no longer works, search for the guideline by name.

## How to use a checklist well

1. **Choose it before you write, not after.** The checklist is a list of what a reader needs. Drafting against it is far easier than retrofitting.
2. **Check the journal's instructions.** Some journals name a specific guideline, extension, or version.
3. **Fill it in honestly.** For each item give the page or section where it is reported. Where an item was not done, say so in the manuscript and explain; do not point to a passage that does not really address it.
4. **Use the explanation document.** Most guidelines publish an "explanation and elaboration" paper with an example of good reporting for every item.
5. **Include the flow diagram** if the guideline has one (CONSORT and PRISMA do).
6. **Submit the completed checklist** as a supplementary file if the journal asks for it.

## What the checklists have in common

Whatever the design, expect to be asked for: the design named in the title or abstract; the objective or hypothesis; who or what was studied and how they were selected; exactly what was measured and how; how the sample size was arrived at; the statistical methods, including how missing data were handled; the numbers at each stage; results with estimates and their precision, not p values alone; limitations; funding and the funder's role; and where the protocol, data, and code can be found.
